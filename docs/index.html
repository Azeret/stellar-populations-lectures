Galaxy clock: race to the [Mg/Fe] target

You decide when your galaxy forms its stars (always 1010 M☉ in total). Press Play and watch the two kinds of supernovae fill the gas with magnesium and iron.

SN II (massive stars) explode within ~40 Myr: lots of Mg, some Fe. SN Ia (white dwarfs) start after ~40 Myr and keep going for Gyr: almost only Fe.

Think about it

1. Why can't anyone beat about +0.46 with a normal IMF, however short the burst?

2. Look at the SN Ia line after star formation stops. Do the stars you already formed notice that iron?

3. Two teams hit +0.10: one formed stars for 2 Gyr, one for 10 Gyr starting late. Can [Mg/Fe] alone tell them apart?

The model in numbers

Per M☉ of stars formed (Kroupa IMF): 0.011 SN II, each 0.11 M☉ Mg + 0.07 M☉ Fe, within 10 Myr; 0.0013 SN Ia, each 0.7 M☉ Fe, spread as 1/t from 40 Myr to 14 Gyr. Gas starts at 3.5×1010 M☉; winds remove 1.5 M☉ of gas per M☉ of stars. Top-heavy IMF: 1.5× more SN II with more Mg per Fe. [Mg/Fe] of your stars = log of (all Mg locked in stars / all Fe locked in stars) relative to the Sun: atoms add up first, then the log. Same physics as notebook 03.
